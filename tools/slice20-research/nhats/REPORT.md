## Q1: is NHATS admissible as the end-to-end oracle leg of DEC-20-9?

**Hidden assumption to check first.** Identical inputs must mean identical physics *and* identical bookkeeping. NHATS has
three bookkeeping choices that the dispatch's hypotheses did not name:
- it publishes the entry speed *after* entry-speed control;
- it books the control dV as a v-inf reduction;
- it does not publish Earth-return v-inf at all.

With those accounted for, agreement is at NHATS's printing precision. Without them, 37 of 77 trajectories disagree by up to 0.99 km/s.

- [Certain] On Horizons states through the unedited kernel, all 77 compared trajectories agree with NHATS within half a unit of NHATS's last printed digit (±0.0005 km/s or km²/s²). This holds for every quantity: C3, Earth-departure v-inf, arrival and departure v-inf, entry speed (uncontrolled), departure dV at 400 km, control dV (model B) and total dV (model B).
  - Every NHATS orbit_id equals the Horizons JPL# solution used (77 of 77).
  - Every leg is M = 0, single branch.
- [Certain] What this shows: Aster's implementation and pipeline — the Lambert solver, the kernel, the v-inf and dV bookkeeping, and the frame, centre and time handling — reproduce an independent two-body patched-conic code to its published precision.
- What this does NOT show: anything about the physics. Both sides are two-body patched conics on the same JPL ephemerides. Agreement cannot be claimed beyond NHATS's 3 decimals: 2 to 5 significant figures, depending on magnitude.
- [Likely] NHATS's calendar dates are 0h TDB (or TT; the difference is under 2 ms). Read as 0h UTC, C3 falls outside rounding in 35 of 77 trajectories. Read as TDB, it is outside in 0.
- [Certain] Bookkeeping differences a K1 oracle must mirror, or it will "disagree":
  1. For the 37 entry-controlled trajectories, NHATS's `v_arr_earth` is the controlled 12.000. Aster's v_EI is the natural speed, up to 12.798 km/s.
  2. NHATS's control dV (`vrel_arr_earth`) equals v-inf minus v-inf(v_EI = 12 at 125 km). This is model B: 37 of 37 within rounding.
     - The pre-registered model A (v_EI − 12) gives 0.38–0.45 times NHATS's value, and its total dV is wrong by up to 0.993 km/s.
     - Model B was identified after seeing the data. It is disclosed as such.
  3. Earth-return v-inf is not published. Inverting the entry speed under DEC-20-8 agrees within the inverted rounding (±0.0005·v_EI/v-inf) for 40 of 40 uncontrolled trajectories. For controlled ones the inversion is meaningless.
- Aster's 200 km parking orbit, against NHATS's 400 km, makes total dV larger by a median 0.027 and at most 0.047 km/s. This is an assumption difference, not an agreement figure.
- Coverage. Only 77 of 200 were compared. All 77 have launch on or after 2026-01-01 and return on or before 2040-12-31, so the sample is biased toward mid-span epochs; see the N2 section. NHATS has no L0 body.

Conclusion [Likely, n = 77]: NHATS is admissible as an implementation and pipeline oracle for heliocentric quantities and entry speed at its 3-decimal precision. Two conditions apply: (a) the oracle compares NHATS's post-control entry speed and model-B control dV, or excludes controlled trajectories, and (b) Earth-return v-inf is derived through a stated convention. It is not an oracle for physics fidelity, because it shares Aster's two-body assumptions.

Strongest counterargument: 77 mid-span trajectories from one JPL code version cannot exclude disagreement near the span edges or on M ≥ 1 legs. Every leg here was M = 0. A single-revolution sample cannot test the multi-revolution paths that K1's return search uses (XR3).

## Q3: is NHATS's 12 km/s limit inertial at 125 km?

- [Certain] Documented, quoted from https://cneos.jpl.nasa.gov/nhats/caveats.html: "Earth atmospheric entry speed less than or equal to 12 km/s at an altitude of 125 km". The page does not say whether the speed is inertial or atmosphere-relative, and it gives no Earth radius. The API doc (https://ssd-api.jpl.nasa.gov/doc/nhats.html) labels `v_arr_earth` only as "Entry Speed (km/s)".
- [Likely] Inertial, at geocentric radius about 6503.14 km (R_E + 125 km):
  - All 40 uncontrolled trajectories match within ±0.0005 km/s; median residual 2.8e-4.
  - Fitting the altitude gives [124.999, 125.012] km from the uncontrolled trajectories, and [124.981, 125.000] km independently from the 37 controlled ones under model B.
  - Rejected, 0 of 40 within rounding:
    - 121.92 km (400,000 ft), 120 km and 130 km;
    - the 6371 km mean radius;
    - atmosphere-relative: polar (0.0096 km/s off), and equatorial eastward or westward with γ −6° (0.47 km/s off).
- R_E 6378.137 against 6378.1366 km: indistinguishable (0.4 m).
- DEC-20-8's assumption matches the inferred convention. The inference rests on N2's v-inf, which agrees with NHATS within rounding on every published quantity.

Strongest counterargument: the fit constrains only the radius at which the inertial speed is evaluated. An atmosphere-relative convention with a different geometry or radius cannot be excluded in general. Only the specific geometries tested are excluded.

## Anything surprising (list only)

- Horizons answers a 100-epoch Earth TLIST (2.3 KB URL) with HTTP 502 four times, while 8-epoch and 12-epoch TLISTs work. The documented limit is 10,000 epochs.
- NHATS's default launch window includes the past: 107 of 200 sampled trajectories launch before the run date, and 98 before 2026-01-01.
- NHATS publishes the *controlled* entry speed (12.000) and books the control as a v-inf reduction.
- Earth-return v-inf is not an NHATS output.
- No L0 body is in NHATS.
- L2 ("no boundary") bodies show position errors up to 7.9 million km and total-dV errors up to 0.81 km/s inside 2026–2046.
- 2026 AM4 is 8.1 million km off, 39 days *inside* its support date.
- The Earth fixture's nodes are bit-identical to today's Horizons (DE441) at all 370 checked epochs.
- NHATS records can be years old: 2000 SG344 was computed 2021-04-19 on solution JPL#17, and 99942 on 2024-10-17 on JPL#220. Horizons still serves the same solutions.
- min_dv and min_dur are the same trajectory for some bodies (for example 420210, 2008 FC and 2021 XF6).
- NHATS's dates behave as 0h TDB, not 0h UTC.
- The dispatch's quoted pilot value 1.5065 is a tie at 4 significant figures.
