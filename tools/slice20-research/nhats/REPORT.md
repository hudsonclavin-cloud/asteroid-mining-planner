## Q1: is NHATS admissible as the end-to-end oracle leg of DEC-20-9?

**Hidden assumption to check first.** Identical inputs must mean identical bookkeeping as well as identical physics. NHATS has three bookkeeping choices the dispatch's hypotheses did not name:
- it publishes the entry speed *after* entry-speed control;
- it books the control dV as a v-inf reduction;
- it does not publish Earth-return v-inf at all.

With those accounted for, agreement is at NHATS's printing precision. Without them, 92 of 200 trajectories disagree, with total dV off by up to 2.03 km/s.

- [Certain] Agreement on Horizons states through the unedited kernel:
  - 1,999 of 2,000 compared values agree with NHATS within half a unit of NHATS's last printed digit (±0.0005 km/s or km²/s²).
  - The quantities are C3, Earth-departure v-inf, arrival and departure v-inf, entry speed (uncontrolled), departure dV at 400 km, control dV (model B), total dV (model B), and the derived Earth-return v-inf.
  - Every NHATS orbit_id equals the Horizons JPL# solution used (200 of 200), and every leg is M = 0, single branch.
- [Certain] The one exception is 101955 (Bennu), min_dur, launching 2042-06-26:
  - C3 21.92410 against NHATS 21.925. That is 0.0009 km²/s², or 4.1e-5 relative, which is 1.8 times the rounding half-unit.
  - Its departure v-inf, departure dV, arrival and departure v-inf all agree within rounding, and total dV agrees to 3.5e-6 km/s. The gap equals about 1e-4 km/s of departure v-inf, which NHATS's 4-s.f. v-inf print (4.682) hides but its 5-s.f. C3 print shows.
  - Cause unknown [Speculative]: a difference in Earth ephemeris or Bennu model 16 years out. Its min_dv trajectory, launching in 2036, agrees to 7e-5.
- [Certain] What this shows: Aster's implementation and pipeline reproduce an independent two-body patched-conic code to its published precision. That covers the Lambert solver, the kernel, the v-inf and dV bookkeeping, and the frame, centre and time handling.
- What this does NOT show: anything about the physics. Both sides use two-body patched conics on the same JPL ephemerides. Agreement cannot be claimed beyond NHATS's 3 decimals, which is 1 to 5 significant figures depending on magnitude.
- [Likely] NHATS's calendar dates are 0h TDB (or TT; the two differ by under 2 ms). Read as 0h UTC, C3 falls outside rounding for 112 of 200 trajectories. Read as TDB, it falls outside for 1.
- [Certain] Bookkeeping that a K1 oracle must mirror, or it will "disagree":
  1. For the 92 entry-controlled trajectories, NHATS's `v_arr_earth` is the controlled 12.000. Aster's v_EI is the natural speed, up to 14.146 km/s.
  2. NHATS's control dV (`vrel_arr_earth`) equals v-inf minus v-inf(v_EI = 12 at 125 km). This is model B, with 92 of 92 within rounding.
     - The pre-registered model A (v_EI − 12) gives 0.38–0.51 times NHATS's control dV and puts total dV off by up to 2.03 km/s.
     - Model B was identified after seeing the first 77 results. It is disclosed as such, and the other 123 then tested it out of sample: 55 more controlled trajectories, all within rounding.
  3. Earth-return v-inf is not published. Inverting the entry speed under DEC-20-8 agrees within the inverted rounding (±0.0005·v_EI/v-inf) for 108 of 108 uncontrolled trajectories. For controlled ones the inversion is meaningless.
- Aster's 200 km parking orbit makes total dV larger than at NHATS's 400 km, by a median 0.024 and at most 0.047 km/s. This is an assumption difference, not an agreement figure.
- Earth inputs: 123 trajectories used fresh Horizons Earth (N2b) and 76 used the Earth fixture's nodes. The fixture's nodes are bit-identical to Horizons at every node checked: 4 in N0 and 366 in N5. At 15 N2 epochs with both available, they differ by at most 7.5e-9 km (floating-point).

Conclusion [Likely, n = 200 trajectories, 100 bodies]: NHATS is admissible as an implementation and pipeline oracle at its 3-decimal precision, on two conditions:
- (a) the oracle compares NHATS's post-control entry speed and model-B control dV, or excludes controlled trajectories;
- (b) Earth-return v-inf is derived through a stated convention.

It is not an oracle for physics fidelity, because it shares Aster's two-body assumptions.

Strongest counterargument: every NHATS leg here is M = 0, so multi-revolution return legs (XR3) are untested. One unexplained C3 outlier, late-epoch and on a body with non-gravitational forces, shows that agreement is not guaranteed at NHATS's finest printed digit.

## Q3: is NHATS's 12 km/s limit inertial at 125 km?

- [Certain] Documented, quoted from https://cneos.jpl.nasa.gov/nhats/caveats.html: "Earth atmospheric entry speed less than or equal to 12 km/s at an altitude of 125 km". No page fetched says inertial or atmosphere-relative, or gives an Earth radius. The API doc (https://ssd-api.jpl.nasa.gov/doc/nhats.html) labels `v_arr_earth` only "Entry Speed (km/s)".
- [Likely] Inertial, at geocentric radius about 6503.137 km (R_E + 125 km):
  - All 108 uncontrolled trajectories match within ±0.0005 km/s; median residual 2.5e-4.
  - The fitted altitude is [124.999, 125.003] km from the uncontrolled trajectories, and independently [124.997, 125.000] km from the 92 controlled ones under model B. The two overlap at 124.999–125.000 km.
  - Widened by N2's own residual scale, the ranges are [124.87, 125.19] and [124.77, 125.22] km.
  - Rejected, with none of 108 within rounding: 121.92 km (400,000 ft), 120 km, 130 km, the 6371 km mean radius, and atmosphere-relative (polar, and equatorial eastward or westward at γ −6°). The controlled test also rejects every atmosphere-relative variant (0 of 92).
- R_E 6378.137 against 6378.1366 km cannot be told apart (0.4 m).
- DEC-20-8's assumption matches the inferred convention.

Strongest counterargument: the fit only constrains the radius at which an inertial speed is evaluated. An atmosphere-relative convention with a geometry or radius not tested here cannot be excluded in general.

## Anything surprising (list only)

- Horizons fails a 100-epoch Earth TLIST (2.3 KB URL) with HTTP 502, but serves 34 of 34 requests at 12 epochs or fewer. The documented limit is 10,000 epochs.
- NHATS's default launch window includes the past: 107 of 200 sampled trajectories launch before the run date, 98 before 2026-01-01.
- NHATS publishes the *controlled* entry speed (12.000) and books the control as a v-inf reduction.
- Earth-return v-inf is not an NHATS output.
- 92 of 200 best trajectories are entry-controlled, with natural entry speeds up to 14.1 km/s.
- One C3 (Bennu, 2042 launch) is 1.8 times NHATS's rounding half-unit away; nothing else is outside rounding.
- No L0 body is in NHATS.
- L2 ("no boundary") bodies show position errors up to 7.9 million km and total-dV errors up to 0.81 km/s inside 2026–2040.
- 2026 AM4 is 8.1 million km off, 39 days *inside* its support date.
- The Earth fixture's nodes are bit-identical to today's Horizons (DE441) at every node checked.
- NHATS records can be years old (2000 SG344 computed 2021-04-19 on JPL#17), yet orbit IDs match Horizons for all 200.
- NHATS's dates behave as 0h TDB, not 0h UTC.
- The dispatch's quoted pilot value 1.5065 is a tie at 4 significant figures.
