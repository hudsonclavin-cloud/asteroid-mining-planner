Model limits and assumptions

[Certain] Catch Δv = |√(v∞² + 2μ/rtip) − (vorb + vtip)|, vis-viva at the upper-tip radius with periapsis placed on the tip.

[Certain] Drop mode deposits exactly the energy one outbound throw withdraws, per kg. The catch and throw states are mirror images.

[Likely] Energy accounting is first-order orbital energy. Spin-energy redistribution and the CoM shift while a payload is attached are neglected.

[Screening only] Plane penalty 2·v∞·sin(Δ/2) assumes the asymptote is rotated far from Earth when |dec| exceeds the facility inclination. Node timing and phasing waits are not modeled. A real catch needs the full v∞ vector, epoch and B-plane target, not declination alone.

[Likely] Entry figures are ballistic Allen–Eggers estimates (H = 7.2 km, interface 120 km). They rank options; they do not size a heat shield. The 5.4 km/s drop entry sits below the 10–16 km/s range of NASA's Earth-entry TPS correlations, so those correlations do not apply.

[Speculative] Hold mode assumes a climber moves the payload to the CoM. That motion is drawn schematically and its timing is invented for the picture. Its energy credit assumes the payload ends at the CoM; retained mass also changes the facility, which is not modeled.

[Speculative] Facility mass defaults to Rev A fiber mass only. Station, ballast and climber mass are not in Rev A. Even so, Rev A fiber alone is ~122× payload, far heavier than MXER's ~10×. The perigee drop comes from the circular orbit, not from too little mass.

[Not modeled] Energy only. Angular momentum and spin recovery are separate budgets and are not reported. Outputs are nominal feasibility, not robust (Monte Carlo) feasibility.

[Likely] Aerocapture is the strongest competitor for both modes. It is not modeled here because its cost is aeroshell mass, not Δv.
