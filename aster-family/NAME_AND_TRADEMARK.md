# "Aster" as the family name — what protects it, who else uses it, what to do

**Status:** research note, leads-grade, written 2026-10-26 from a web research pass with URL-cited sources. It is not legal advice. The one action it recommends before anything else is free: talk to a law-school trademark clinic.

## §1. The correction: a name cannot be copyrighted

- [Certain] The U.S. Copyright Office: "Copyright does not protect names, titles, slogans, or short phrases … Some names may be protected under trademark law." (copyright.gov FAQ.) Copyright already protects, automatically and without filing, the things you have actually written: the planner's source, the Skyhook physics module and docs, the founding documents, the site text. Registering that copyright is cheap and optional and only matters if you ever need to sue.
- [Certain] What protects a **name** is trademark. In the US, trademark rights begin with **use**, not filing, and they are limited to the goods/services and geography where you use the mark. You may put **™** (not ®) on Aster today with no filing. ® is only for federally registered marks.
- [Certain] Trademarks are tied to goods and services, not to the word in the abstract. Identical marks coexist for unrelated goods (the USPTO's own example is Dove soap and Dove ice cream). Marks need not be identical to conflict when the goods are related.
- [Certain] You already have dated evidence of first use in the mission-planning niche: `aster-mission-mcp` published on npm 2026-07-10, the site title "Aster V2 — Solar System", and the Pages deploy history.

## §3. What a real clearance looks at

- **Class 9** (downloadable software: the MCP server, CLI tools, a downloadable parts app) and **Class 42** (hosted software, engineering services, design of parts for others). File these two, nothing else, until physical goods exist.
- **Not now:** Classes 7/12/13/37 become relevant only if tether hardware or engines are ever sold or serviced as physical goods.
- **Fees** [Certain, USPTO schedule effective 2025-01-19]: $350 per class for an electronic application that uses ID-Manual wording (+$200/class if you write free-text goods descriptions, so don't). Two classes = $700. Intent-to-use adds $150/class at the Statement of Use and $125/class per six-month extension.
- **Filing basis:** §1(a) use-in-commerce is arguably available now for the planner (confirm with the clinic that free distribution counts as use for your goods); §1(b) intent-to-use for the family extensions not yet launched.
- **Owner trap** [Likely, Lanham Act §10]: an intent-to-use application generally cannot be assigned to a different owner before use is alleged. Decide **before** filing whether you personally or a future entity owns the mark. The family master plan already says incorporation waits for a real trigger (first customer, contract, or equity question); that is consistent with filing in your own name now and assigning later only if use has been alleged.
- **State registration:** skip it. It gives no ® and does not reach beyond the state.
- **Europe:** a separate question with a different landscape (MBDA, Aster Capital, ASTER Technologies). EU rights are first-to-file; US use buys nothing there.

## §4. Name forms, ranked

1. **Aster Orbital** — keeps the word, adds the field, separates from LogicMark's app and from ASTER Labs' bare name. Strongest candidate found. Clear before use.
2. **Asterworks** — one distinctive word; reads as a works/forge, fits PartForge and the tether program; easy to own as a GitHub org and npm scope. Clear before use.
3. **Aster Mission Systems** — accurate for a family built around mission planning, tether mission design and parts for missions; long but defensible.
4. **Aster Astrodynamics** — narrows the comparison set to the actual competence; technical.
5. Bare **Aster** — keep it as the *product* name of the planner (where you already have use) even if the *house* takes a compound form.

**Avoid:** "Aster Labs" (two active companies, one in your exact field), "Aster Space" (Indian aircraft-engine-parts firm plus the TU/e team), "Aster Technologies" (Siemens).

**Fresh alternatives with the same feel, if clearance fails:** Starwort (the aster flower's English name), Periapsis Works, Caelum Works, Vesper Orbital, Tessera Space. All unverified; clear before use.

**Narrative footnote:** Ateliers de Construction Mécanique l'Aster (France, c. 1890s–1912) mass-produced engines and licensed aircraft engines in WWI. Defunct, zero legal weight, but a real historical "Aster" engine brand is a good story for a family that includes an engine-design tool.

## §5. Recommended path, in order

1. **Stop saying "copyright the name."** Put **Aster™** on the planner's README, site footer and npm description now; it costs nothing and starts a consistent record. Add a dated first-use note (2026-07-10 npm publish) to the hub.
2. **Run the real clearance search** at tmsearch.uspto.gov: `CM:aster*` with IC:009 and IC:042, then sound-alikes, then owner searches (Teradata, Aster Labs Inc, LogicMark). Then a common-law sweep (web, app stores, npm, GitHub, Crunchbase). Record results in a dated decision record, the way Skyhook records physics decisions.
3. **Use a law-school clinic.** The USPTO Law School Clinic Certification Program lists clinics that draft and file trademark applications pro bono under supervision; filter for Trademarks near you. Bring this note, the TSDR printout for Reg. 8043177, and the ASTER Labs product pages.
4. **Decide form and owner** (§3 trap) before filing. Preference: a compound house mark (Aster Orbital / Asterworks) over bare Aster.
5. **File Classes 9 and 42** with ID-Manual wording (e.g. Class 9 "downloadable software for planning and analyzing spacecraft trajectories and asteroid missions"; Class 42 "providing temporary use of online non-downloadable software for spacecraft mission planning; engineering services in the field of spacecraft and propulsion design").
6. **Lock the footprint** for the chosen compound in one sitting: domain, GitHub organization (move the repos under it), npm scope, handles. Never any "asterlabs" form.
7. **Put a LICENSE in every repo** (Skyhook, Idea-Bank and the suborbital stub have none) with "© 2026 Hudson Clavin". An unlicensed public repo is all-rights-reserved by default, which contradicts the open posture the READMEs take.
8. **Watch** quarterly for new ASTER filings in Classes 9/42; your dated July 2026 use is your priority evidence if anyone files for space software first.
